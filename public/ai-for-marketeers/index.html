Why we're here

Before we touch a single tool — let's talk about the two traps most teams fall into.

⚠️

Trap #1 — AI for the sake of AI

Using AI because it feels like you should. No clear goal, no defined use case, no way to measure if it's actually helping. The output is mediocre and nobody knows why.

⚠️

Trap #2 — Becoming dependent without understanding

Letting AI make decisions you can't verify. Copy-pasting outputs without knowing what went in. Building workflows on top of tools you've never properly configured. One update and everything breaks.

✦

The DIY principle

Understanding the machine is what protects you — creatively, strategically, professionally. Today isn't about using more AI. It's about using it better, with full control over what goes in and what comes out.

The three layers of AI use

Most people stay at layer one. Today we move you to layer two — and show you what layer three looks like.

💬

Layer 1 — Ad hoc chat

One-off prompts. No memory. No context between sessions. Starts from scratch every time. What 80% of teams do.

🎯

Layer 2 — Custom assistant

Built once, reused infinitely. Knows your brand, your tone, your rules. Consistent output without re-briefing every time.

🤖

Layer 3 — Agent / Swarm

Acts autonomously. Multi-step workflows. Connects to tools. Multiple specialists collaborating. The frontier — and it's closer than you think.

💡

Today's goal

Move from Layer 1 → Layer 2. Understand Layer 3 well enough to know when it's relevant for your team and your clients.

Prompt Basics & Anatomy

The fundamentals — fast and sharp. Plus the techniques most people never discover.

What is a prompt?

A prompt is an instruction to someone who knows everything about the world — but nothing about your context, your brand, or your goals.

🍳

Think of it like cooking

You can give a professional chef every ingredient in the world. If you don't tell them what dish you want, what occasion it's for, and how your guests eat — they'll cook something technically correct and completely wrong for you.

📋

Or a new hire on day one

Brilliant person. Zero context about your agency, your clients, your tone. The quality of their first week depends entirely on the quality of your onboarding. Same principle applies to every AI interaction.

The perfect prompt — anatomy

Every high-quality prompt has the same six layers. Miss one and the output suffers.

ROLE
Who is the AI? Not just "copywriter" — give seniority, specialisation, and perspective. "You are a senior B2B content strategist with 10 years in digital marketing agencies."
CONTEXT
What's the situation? Brand, audience, platform, goal. Don't dump everything — prioritise. Strategy first, then audience, then value prop, then differentiators.
TASK
What exactly should it do? Be precise. "Write a LinkedIn post" vs "Write 3 LinkedIn post variations — one benefit-driven, one pain-focused, one curiosity hook."
TONE
How should it sound? Not "professional" — behavioural rules. "Write like a trusted colleague, not a press release. Avoid buzzwords. Lead with the benefit."
FORMAT
What does the output look like? Define structure explicitly: headline (3 options), hook (2 sentences), 3 bullets, CTA. If you don't specify — AI decides.
CONSTRAINTS
What should it never do? Forbidden phrases, compliance rules, tone boundaries. The AI can only follow rules it knows.
✦ Master Prompt Template
You are a [ROLE + SENIORITY + SPECIALISATION]. // Context layer — ordered by priority Brand: [brand name + what they do + positioning] Audience: [who they are, what they care about, their language] Goal: [what this content should achieve] Platform: [where this will live] // Task [Specific task with exact deliverable] // Tone rules DO: [specific behaviours — e.g. lead with benefit, conversational] DON'T: [forbidden phrases, styles, tones] // Output format Deliver: - [element 1 — e.g. Headline (3 options)] - [element 2 — e.g. Hook paragraph (2 sentences max)] - [element 3 — e.g. 3 key points, benefit-driven] - [element 4 — e.g. CTA] // If anything is unclear, ask before writing.
Next level — what most people skip

These techniques separate users from builders.

🔍

Reverse Engineering

Point AI at a competitor's website, ad, or content — and ask it to extract the brand voice, positioning, implied values, and communication strategy. Instant competitive intelligence.

🌐

Scraping & Analysis

Feed Claude a URL or paste raw content. Ask it to analyse patterns, extract tone, cluster themes, or identify what's missing. Audience research in minutes instead of days.

⚡

Agentic Mode

Let AI take multi-step actions autonomously: browse the web, run analyses, compile reports. You set the goal — it figures out the steps. Available in Claude, ChatGPT, and Perplexity.

📖

Brand Book Building

Feed AI your existing content (5–10 pieces). Ask it to extract and codify your brand voice, recurring adjectives, content patterns, and tone rules into a reusable system prompt.

Building Custom AI

The difference between a tool that frustrates you and one that works like a senior team member.

💡

The hiring analogy

Write your assistant's job description the same way you'd write a vacancy: function title, responsibilities, tone expectations, and — crucially — what you don't want. That mental shift forces you to be concrete. An assistant with no defined scope has no character.

The honest answer

None of these know your brand. Whichever tool you use — enrichment is mandatory. The platform is secondary to the quality of what you put in.

The 5 building blocks of a system prompt
ROLE

❌ "You are a copywriter AI"

No seniority, no expertise, no depth

✓ "You are a senior content strategist with 10 years in B2B marketing, specialising in..."

Gives the model a level to perform at

TONE

❌ "Write in a warm tone"

The model interprets "warm" its own way

✓ "Write like a trusted colleague — no jargon, no press release formality, always lead with empathy"

Behavioural rules + example sentences

CONTEXT

❌ "Here's everything about our brand… write something engaging"

AI can't identify what matters most

✓ Ordered: Strategy → Audience → Value prop → Differentiators → Good examples

Each layer builds on the previous

RULES

❌ "Follow our forbidden word list"

Without the actual list — it improvises

✓ Include the actual list. Embed the content, not a reference to it.

Rules it doesn't know, it can't follow

FORMAT

❌ "Give me a text"

AI picks the structure. 50% chance it's wrong.

✓ "Headline (3 options), Hook (2 sentences), 3 bullets, CTA, Meta description (155 chars)"

Control the structure or AI will

The 8 most common mistakes
01

The mistake

Too broad of scope. "It should be able to write copy, set strategy, translate, make briefings, spot trends…"

The fix

Pick one core function. Build from there. A specialist always outperforms a generalist.

02

The mistake

Instructions without tone examples. "Write warmly" is a wish, not an instruction.

The fix

Always include example sentences — both correct AND incorrect. The AI learns from contrast.

03

The mistake

Referring to rules without including them. "See our brand guidelines" — the AI has never seen them.

The fix

Embed the content, not the instruction to follow it. Enrich with substance, not references.

04

The mistake

Locking in trend sources. Those references age fast and limit the assistant's strategic range.

The fix

User brings current trend info into the session prompt. The assistant interprets it — not stores it.

05

The mistake

Never testing edge cases. Works on standard tasks — breaks on anything unexpected.

The fix

Deliberately test with ambiguous, incomplete, or unusual inputs before you ship it to the team.

06

The mistake

One assistant for NL and FR. Bilingual by design — mediocre in both languages.

The fix

Build separate assistants per language. Each has different CTA conventions, rhythm, and vocabulary.

07

The mistake

Treating AI as a replacement. Letting it decide things you haven't validated.

The fix

You set direction. AI executes and accelerates. Human always in the lead — not the loop.

08

The mistake

No maintenance after launch. Brands evolve. The assistant doesn't know unless you update it.

The fix

Schedule quarterly reviews. An outdated assistant is a compliance and quality risk.

Advanced prompting techniques

Embed these into your system prompts or use them ad hoc.

🧠 Chain of Thought (CoT)
Before you answer, think through this step by step. Show your reasoning process, state your assumptions clearly, and flag where you are uncertain before giving your final output.
🎯 ADHD-Friendly Output
Format this content for someone with ADHD: - Start every section with the conclusion, not the build-up - Max 2 sentences per paragraph - Bold the single most important phrase in each section - No introductory preamble — begin immediately with the substance - Use clear visual breaks between sections
🔄 Socratic Intake (make AI ask first)
Before you write anything, ask me the questions you need to produce the best possible output for this task. Keep it to a maximum of 5 questions, prioritised by impact. Wait for my answers before you begin writing.

Claude Deep Dive

Skills, Projects, Agents — and why a swarm of specialists beats one generalist every time.

The three Claude modes
💬

Chat

One-off. No memory between sessions. Great for experiments and one-time tasks. Starts from zero every time.


USE WHEN: quick, exploratory, non-repeating

📁

Projects

Persistent system instructions + document uploads. Shares knowledge across your team. On-brand every time without re-briefing.


USE WHEN: repeating tasks, team workflows, brand consistency

🤖

Agents / Skills

Autonomous multi-step execution. Connects to tools and APIs. A Skill is a saved expert role. An Agent acts independently.


USE WHEN: automation, complex workflows, no human per step

The Swarm Principle

The most common mistake: "Let's build one Content AI and we're done." The reality: you need specialists.

❌ The Generalist Trap

One assistant that writes copy, sets strategy, translates, builds briefings, and monitors trends. It does all of these things — averagely. No depth, no consistent voice, constant re-prompting.

✓ The Specialist Swarm

Multiple focused assistants, each with deep domain knowledge in their area. They share the same brand foundation but each has its own expertise. One brand voice. Many specialisms.

Swarm questionnaire — start here

1. What content do you create most often? (Top 3)
2. What is your #1 content bottleneck right now?
3. What is your niche or domain expertise?
4. Do you have a defined brand voice?

Your answers define which specialist assistant you build first.

Pre-build checklist

Before you write a single line of system prompt — answer these 10 questions.

  • 1. How does the assistant gather information — does it ask, or assume?
  • 2. Does it model expert reasoning, or just execute tasks?
  • 3. Does it lead the user, or follow instructions?
  • 4. What is the flow and hierarchy of information it receives?
  • 5. What does "good output" look like — can you describe it exactly?
  • 6. How does it handle memory gaps between sessions?
  • 7. Is it structured around modes or single workflows?
  • 8. How does it handle vague or incomplete prompts?
  • 9. What brand, domain, and ethical safeguards are embedded?
  • 10. When and how should it push back on the user?
04

ChatGPT & Co-Pilot

What's actually in these tools — beyond what you already know.

ChatGPT — beyond basic chat
🔧

Custom GPTs

Pre-configured assistants for specific tasks. Set instructions and knowledge once — reuse forever. Best for: proposals, mailing content, procurement screening, repeating briefing workflows.


Trigger: when you're repeating the same type of prompt in the same context.

📂

Projects

Persistent memory + file uploads per project context. Similar to Claude Projects but with different sharing constraints. Team access requires Enterprise or Team plan.

⚡

Agentic / Operator Mode

ChatGPT can browse, execute code, fill forms, and chain multi-step tasks autonomously. Deep Research builds full reports with cited sources. Excellent for competitive analysis and audience research at scale.

🎨

Canvas

Co-edit documents and code directly in the interface. Iterates visually. Great for long-form documents where you want to refine alongside the AI rather than prompt → copy → paste.

⚠️

Memory — powerful and risky

ChatGPT's memory feature retains information about you across conversations. Useful when set intentionally. Risky when you've forgotten what it remembers. Audit it regularly: Settings → Memory → Manage.

Co-Pilot — the embedded option

Co-Pilot's advantage is where it lives — inside the tools you already use.

✅

Where Co-Pilot wins

  • Meeting notes → action items in Teams (seconds)
  • Excel data → summaries and visualisations
  • Email drafts in Outlook — contextual and quick
  • PowerPoint from a Word doc — automated structure
  • No context-switching — stays in your workflow
⚠️

Where Co-Pilot falls short

  • Creative and brand-nuanced copy
  • Long-form strategic content
  • Custom system instructions (very limited)
  • Multi-language or brand voice precision
  • Complex prompt engineering
✦

Practical rule of thumb

Co-Pilot for operational efficiency in M365. Claude for brand-critical and nuanced content. ChatGPT for research, versatility, and agentic tasks. They're not competitors — they're a stack.

Visual AI

What works, what doesn't, what's replaced what — and how to use it without embarrassing yourself or your clients.

⚠️

The Sora situation

OpenAI's Sora was positioned as the video AI revolution — but access remains limited and inconsistent. For most agencies, it's not yet a reliable production tool. What's actually usable right now is elsewhere.

Tool landscape — what each does well

Midjourney

Image · Strong
  • Best quality for editorial, lifestyle, and concept imagery
  • Strong aesthetic control with style references
  • Not ideal for exact product rendering
  • No native text accuracy — avoid text in images
  • Prompt: style + subject + lighting + mood + aspect ratio

Adobe Firefly

Image · Safe
  • Commercially safe — trained on licensed content only
  • Deep Photoshop integration (generative fill, expand)
  • Strong for product retouching and background generation
  • Less creative range than Midjourney for concept work
  • Best for: client-facing, legally safe deliverables

DALL·E (ChatGPT)

Image · Medium
  • Improved with GPT-4o — much better text in images
  • Good for quick mockups and concepts in workflow
  • Not production-level for brand imagery
  • Useful for: presentation visuals, ideation boards
  • Easy to iterate within the same ChatGPT conversation

Canva AI

Design · Accessible
  • Best for non-designers who need branded social output fast
  • Magic Resize, AI copy, background removal all solid
  • Limited creative ceiling — recognisable "Canva look"
  • Strong for: templates, social posts, quick decks
  • Not for: premium creative or complex brand work

Kling / Runway

Video · Growing
  • Kling: strong short-form video from image or text prompt
  • Runway Gen-3: better temporal consistency than earlier versions
  • Both are viable for social video content (6–10 sec clips)
  • Still struggles with complex motion and hands
  • Best for: product lifestyle video, social ads, loop content

Envato / Stock AI

Assets · Safe
  • AI-generated stock that's commercially licensed
  • Good for filler imagery and social backgrounds
  • Not brand-specific by nature
  • Useful for: pitch decks, internal comms, ad testing
  • Combine with Firefly for brand-adapted output
What visual AI can and cannot replace
Task AI can replace AI cannot replace
Product photography Lifestyle context shots, background variants, scale tests Exact product details, packaging text, highly regulated categories

Do's & Don'ts — Visual AI

✓ Do

  • Always disclose AI-generated imagery where required by platform or regulation
  • Use AI for iteration and ideation — human for final creative direction
  • Verify commercial licensing before client use (Firefly = safest)
  • Run AI images through post-production — they rarely ship raw
  • Use reference images for brand consistency (style, colour, mood)
  • Test multiple tools for the same task — results vary significantly

✗ Don't

  • Use AI-generated faces of real, recognisable people
  • Generate imagery of minors without explicit purpose and caution
  • Pass AI imagery as photography without disclosure to clients
  • Rely on AI for accurate text within images (still unreliable)
  • Use Midjourney/DALL-E for regulated sectors (pharma, finance, legal) without legal review
  • Skip the copyright check — AI training data is still legally contested

Do's, Don'ts & Maturity

The guardrails — and an honest look at where most teams actually are.

✓ Do

  • Always keep a human in the lead — not just the loop
  • Treat first drafts as first drafts — iterate-first mindset
  • Embed content in system prompts, not references to it
  • Schedule quarterly reviews of all assistants
  • Document every system prompt iteration and why you changed it
  • Test edge cases and unusual inputs before sharing with the team
  • Use separate language assistants for NL and FR
  • Specify output format — always

✗ Don't

  • Let AI fabricate stats, testimonials, or claims — label hypotheticals
  • Input confidential client data into public tools (GDPR risk)
  • Build one assistant to do everything
  • Let AI make final decisions on brand or compliance matters
  • Assume "professional tone" is a sufficient instruction
  • Launch an assistant and never update it
  • Treat outputs as final without a human editorial pass
  • Use AI imagery in regulated sectors without legal review

Action Plan

Three things you do this week. Not someday — this week.

Day 1 — Pick one repeating task

Identify one task you do weekly that follows the same pattern. A specific type of copy, a briefing format, a report structure. That's your first assistant.

Day 2 — Write the system prompt

Use the template from this session. Role + Context + Tone rules (with examples) + Do's & Don'ts + Output format. Keep it under 8,000 characters. Focus beats completeness.

Day 3 — Test with edge cases

Don't just test the easy tasks. Give it something ambiguous, something incomplete, something off-script. Find where it breaks before your team or clients do.

Week 6 — Review and iterate

Schedule a 30-minute review now. What changed in your brand? What did the assistant get wrong repeatedly? Update the system prompt. Document what you changed and why.
